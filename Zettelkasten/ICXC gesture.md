# ICXC gesture

Translation from [[@didronIconographieChretienneHistoire1843]] (see below)
"This hand gives the Latin blessing and not the Greek blessing. In our country [France] The direction of the fingers does not seem symbolic, while among the Greeks the index finger lengthens like an I, the big finger curves like a C, ancient sigma of the Greeks, the thumb and ring finger cross to make an X. and the little finger rounds to represent a C. All this gives IC-XC, Greek monogram of Jesus Christ (Ιησούς Χριστός)."

"This hand gives the Latin blessing and not the Greek blessing. Among us, the orientation of the fingers does not seem symbolic, whereas among the Greeks the index finger extends like an I, the middle finger bends like a C, the thumb and the ring finger cross to form an X, and the little finger curves to form a G. Taken together, this gives IC-XC, the Greek monogram of Jesus Christ (Ιησοῦς Χριστός).

This hand, sculpted in a tympanum of Ferrara Cathedral, by its position seems to belong to a figure on the ground, who, from on high, blesses those placed below. On page 32, pl. 20, we gave an example of a hand in a quite different position—descending from heaven instead of rising from the earth. On the west portal of Sens Cathedral, a divine hand, set against a cruciferous nimbus, also descends from heaven and, from the summit of the arch, blesses an entire band of martyrs climbing upward, to the right and left, along the branches of the ogive."
